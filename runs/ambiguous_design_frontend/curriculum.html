Assumptions I made
  • 'Design' means UI/visual design for web apps, not graphic design, UX research, or brand design
  • The learner wants to improve the look of interfaces they build in code, so developer-oriented design content fits best
  • Because they know CSS, layout mechanics are skipped and the focus is on design decisions
  • 'Nothing older than 2020' means videos published in 2020 or later
  • No design tool such as Figma is assumed; tool basics are included only as far as needed to follow redesigns
  1. Visual hierarchy and layout composition
    They can build any layout with Flexbox and Grid, but need to learn how to guide the eye with size, weight, contrast and emphasis.
  2. Spacing, alignment and consistent spacing scales
    Inconsistent spacing is the most common reason developer-built UIs look amateur, and a spacing scale maps directly onto CSS variables.
  3. Typography for interfaces
    Font choice, type scale, line height and line length strongly affect polish and readability and are easy to apply in CSS.
  4. Color systems, contrast and accessibility
    They need a practical way to build palettes, shades and accessible contrast rather than picking colors by guesswork.
  5. Polish details: shadows, borders, radius, and icons
    Small refinements make UIs feel finished, and they translate directly into CSS properties.
  6. Critiquing existing UIs and spotting problems
    Learning to diagnose what is wrong with a real interface builds the design eye and satisfies the need for practical critique.
  7. Full redesign of a real UI, step by step
    Watching principles applied end to end on a real interface shows how to combine them and gives a repeatable process.
