# Curriculum: Bake my first sourdough loaf this weekend; my starter is already active

**Budget:** 120 min · **Planned:** 87 min · **Videos:** 5 · **Candidates assessed:** 36

**How I read the goal:** Bake a first sourdough boule this weekend with an already-active starter, following one full bake-along, with extra help on shaping and common beginner errors, using at most two bakers.

**Assumptions:** Bread Code (Hendrik) is the main baker for the method; Mike G (LifebyMikeG) is the second baker, used for mistakes and cautions only.; Where the two differ (for example Mike G's 50% whole-wheat recipe and 30-minute fold intervals), follow the Bread Code recipe and use Mike G for the principles.; Learner has a banneton or can use a bowl with a towel, and a Dutch oven or a similar covered pot.
> ⚠️ Bread Code's full course was chosen over Brian Lagerstrom's and Grant Bakes' bake-alongs because it explains the reasons behind each step, including cold retard and crumb diagnosis. Those two are near-duplicates and would add a third baker.
> ⚠️ Only the Bread Code and Mike G methods are used, which meets the two-baker limit. Do not mix in other creators.
> ⚠️ Total is about 87 of 120 minutes. The rest is left unused to avoid padding with redundant videos. The bake itself takes about 24 hours, with waiting time outside viewing time.
> ⚠️ The shaping video shows a second, advanced stitching method. Skip it on your first loaf.

## Plan

- **Core concepts: hydration, levain, fermentation** (`concepts`): Hydration and levain are new to the learner. Understanding them makes the recipe make sense.
- **Beginner mistakes to avoid** (`mistakes`): The learner asked for help with the steps beginners get wrong.
- **Full bake-along: levain, mix, bulk and folds, shape, cold retard, score, Dutch oven** (`bakealong`): This is the main thing to follow during the bake.
- **Pre-shaping and shaping a boule** (`shaping`): Shaping is the hardest hands-on skill. A closer look at it supports the bake-along.

It goes through 15 beginner errors in order, including an inactive starter, autolyse, the stretch-and-fold cadence, and the poke test for proofing. It is the second baker, used for cautions.

> [00:47] “having a really healthy active sourdough starter this is the life force of your bread this is the yeast”

_Confidence note: His recipe is 50% whole wheat and differs from the Bread Code recipe, so use his tips and not his recipe._
