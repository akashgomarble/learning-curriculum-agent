Plan

Assumptions I made
  • The 'weekend' bake means mixing Friday/Saturday, a cold retard overnight, and baking the next day, so a 1-2 day timeline is acceptable.
  • The learner owns or can get a Dutch oven, a kitchen scale, and a banneton or a bowl with a towel.
  • The learner wants a standard country-style white or lightly whole-wheat boule at around 70-75% hydration, not an advanced high-hydration loaf.
  • Since 'at most two bakers' methods' is required, the plan favors one primary full bake-along baker plus one optional secondary for troubleshooting.
  1. Hydration and building a levain from an active starter
    They know yeasted dough but not baker's percentages or how to turn a starter into a levain that fits the weekend schedule.
  2. Mixing, autolyse, and bulk fermentation with stretch-and-folds
    Yeast timing cues do not apply; they must learn to read dough (volume increase, bubbles, jiggle) instead of the clock, and replace kneading with folds.
  3. Pre-shaping and shaping a boule with surface tension
    Sandwich-loaf shaping is different; wetter sourdough needs tension-building technique to hold its shape.
  4. Final proof and cold retard in the fridge
    Makes the weekend schedule workable and develops flavor; they need to know how to judge proofing with the poke test and avoid overproofing.
  5. Scoring and baking in a preheated Dutch oven
    Dutch oven steam and scoring control oven spring and crust, which they have never done with sandwich bread.
  6. Follow a full beginner sourdough bake-along from start to finish
    Ties all steps together into one consistent method so the learner can follow along at the counter.
  7. Common beginner sourdough mistakes and fixes
    Helps diagnose dense crumb, flat loaves, sticking dough, and underproofing/overproofing on a first bake.
