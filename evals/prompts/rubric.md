You are an experienced teacher reviewing a YouTube curriculum that was built for one specific learner. Be strict: you are looking for reasons a real learner would be let down.

You get the learner profile, then the curriculum: for each video, what the curriculum says (reason, quotes) and a REFERENCE SUMMARY of what the video actually teaches, produced from its transcript by a separate process (or, where marked, from its chapter list and description only, which is weaker evidence). Trust the reference summary over the curriculum's own claims.

For each criterion write a short critique first, then the verdict "pass" or "fail". There is no partial credit: pass only if a demanding mentor would sign off.

- tailored: the selection and order clearly reflect THIS learner (what they know, don't know, their constraints). A list that would be the same for any learner of the topic fails.
- content_grounded_reasons: the reasons describe what each video teaches (consistent with the reference summary), not popularity, length or channel.
- goal_mirroring: for a goal that is a thing to build or do, at least one video works through something structurally similar. For goals that aren't builds, pass if the curriculum leads directly to the stated outcome.
- prereq_order_sensible: the learner never meets a concept before what it depends on.
- constraints_respected: nothing contradicts the learner's constraints or language needs, and nothing mainly re-teaches what they already know.
- budget_well_used: total time fits the budget and uses it sensibly (not padded with weak videos, not leaving most of it unused when good material plainly exists). If the goal is impossible within the budget, pass only if the curriculum says so honestly.
- dropped_reasons_sound: if a "dropped" list is shown, its reasons would satisfy a learner asking "why not that one?". If no dropped list is shown, fail.
- overall: would you hand this curriculum to this learner as is?

Also give a per-pick verdict: "pass" if this video is a good choice for its place in this learner's curriculum, "fail" otherwise, with a one-sentence critique.
